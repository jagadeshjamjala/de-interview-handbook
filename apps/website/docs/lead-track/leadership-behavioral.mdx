# Leadership behavioral questions

The format is the same as the [behavioral round](../rounds/behavioral.mdx), but the bar is **scope and influence**. A senior story about fixing a pipeline becomes a lead story when it changes how several teams work, and you can show the effect.

<LevelExpectations
  senior="Have stories where your decision affected other teams, and where you led without formal authority."
  lead="Every story shows organization-level scope: direction you set, people you brought along or grew, trade-offs you owned, and measured outcomes."
/>

## The stories to have ready

| Theme | Example prompt | What they're testing |
|---|---|---|
| **Setting direction** | "Tell me about a technical strategy you defined." | Diagnosis, prioritization, saying no |
| **Influence without authority** | "Tell me about getting another team to change how they work." | Understanding their incentives, building a coalition |
| **Disagreement with leadership** | "When did you push back on a decision from above?" | Judgment, courage, committing after the decision |
| **A hard trade-off** | "Tell me about a decision with no good option." | Making the trade-off explicit, owning the outcome |
| **Failure** | "Tell me about a project that failed." | Honesty, what you changed afterwards |
| **Growing people** | "Tell me about someone you helped grow." | Coaching, delegation, giving stretch work |
| **Delivering through others** | "Tell me about a large project you led across teams." | Planning, unblocking, communication |
| **Managers: performance** | "Tell me about an underperforming team member." | Fairness, clarity, follow-through |

One story can cover two themes, but have at least six distinct stories so you don't repeat yourself across a loop.

## Telling a lead-level story

1. **Context with stakes:** the business problem and why it mattered, in one or two sentences with a number.
2. **What made it hard:** competing priorities, other teams' goals, missing information.
3. **Your decisions:** what you chose and what you chose *not* to do, and why. Say "I" for your decisions and "we" for the team's work.
4. **How you brought people along:** who needed convincing, what they cared about, how you got agreement.
5. **The result in numbers**, and what you'd do differently.

Keep each story to about three minutes, then let the interviewer ask follow-ups. They'll dig into the parts that test the competency they're scoring.

## Common traps

- Stories at senior-IC scope: a great pipeline fix that affected nobody else.
- "We" throughout, so the interviewer can't tell what you decided.
- No result, or only activity ("we had many meetings") instead of outcomes.
- Blaming other teams or leadership; show how you handled the situation instead.
