# Team design and hiring

Manager and lead loops almost always include "How would you structure the team?" and "How do you hire?". Staff-level ICs get lighter versions ("How would you organize ownership of the platform?"). Strong answers start from the problems the team must solve, not from an org chart.

## Team structures

| Model | Works well when | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Central data team** | Small company, shared platform still being built | Turns into a ticket queue; far from business context |
| **Embedded** in product or business teams | Domains need deep context and fast iteration | Duplicated tooling and models; isolated engineers with no peers |
| **Hybrid: platform team + domain-aligned engineers** | Mid-size and up | Needs clear ownership boundaries and a shared practice (reviews, standards) |
| **Data mesh** (domains own data products) | Large organizations with mature domains and a strong self-serve platform | Requires real platform investment and governance; easy to name, hard to do |

Make ownership explicit: who owns each source, core model, dashboard and on-call rotation. Shared things with no owner decay.

## Sequencing hires

- **Hire against the biggest problem.** A fragile platform blocks everyone, so stabilizing it often comes first.
- **Seniors before juniors** in a new team, so someone can set standards, review designs and mentor.
- **A manager or tech lead** once a team grows past roughly 6–8 people, or sooner if the lead is overloaded with both delivery and people work.
- Balance specialties to the work: platform, analytics engineering, streaming or ML data, as the roadmap needs them.

## Running hiring

1. **Scorecard first:** the competencies and level expectations for the role, written before the job is posted.
2. **A structured loop:** each interviewer covers specific competencies with prepared questions and a rubric. Use realistic exercises (SQL on real-looking data, modeling a business process, a design discussion) instead of puzzles.
3. **Independent feedback** written before the debrief, so the loudest voice doesn't decide.
4. **Decide against the scorecard**, not against "would I like to work with them".
5. **Measure it:** time to hire, offer acceptance, and how hires perform after six months.

## Performance and growth

Expect at least one question about an underperformer or a conflict. Show that you:

- Set clear expectations early and give specific, timely feedback.
- Look for causes (unclear role, skills gap, personal circumstances) before concluding.
- Agree on a concrete improvement plan with support and check-ins, and document it.
- Act when it doesn't work, fairly and respectfully, and with HR involved where needed.

For growth: career ladders with examples of each level, stretch projects matched to people's goals, and promotion cases built from evidence over time.
