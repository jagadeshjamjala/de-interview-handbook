# Behavioral round

Behavioral rounds decide your **level** more often than people think. Interviewers use your stories to judge scope: how big the problems were, how much you owned, and how many people your decisions affected.

<LevelExpectations
  junior="Stories about learning fast, owning your tasks, asking for help well, and a mistake you handled honestly."
  mid="Stories where you owned a pipeline or project end to end, handled an incident, and worked through a disagreement."
  senior="Stories where you led a multi-person effort, raised the bar for the team (tests, contracts, on-call), and influenced without authority."
  lead="Stories about direction across teams, hiring and growing people, hard trade-offs with leadership, and organization-level results."
/>

## The answer shape

STAR (situation, task, action, result) works. For data engineering, add two things interviewers specifically look for: **impact in numbers** and **what changed afterwards**.

1. **Context (15 seconds).** The system, its scale, and why it mattered. "Our nightly revenue pipeline feeds the CFO's 7am dashboard."
2. **The problem or goal**, with a number. "It had missed its SLA six times that month."
3. **What *you* did.** Use "I" for your actions and "we" for the team's. Name the specific technical and people steps.
4. **Result,** measured. "Zero missed SLAs for the next two quarters; runtime 6h → 50min."
5. **What changed or what you learned.** The prevention, the process, the habit.

Aim for **2–3 minutes** per story, then let the interviewer dig in. Have the details ready (tools, numbers, who disagreed and why), because the follow-ups are where they really assess you.

## Build a story bank

Prepare **six to eight stories** in advance and map them to common questions. One good story usually answers two or three different prompts.

| Story type | Answers questions about |
|---|---|
| **An incident or data quality failure you owned** | ownership, pressure, debugging, mistakes |
| **A migration or big rebuild** | planning, de-risking, leading a project |
| **A metric definition dispute** | stakeholders, conflict, influence |
| **Shipping under time pressure** | trade-offs, prioritization, saying no |
| **A performance or cost win** | technical depth, impact |
| **Learning a new domain or system fast** | learning, onboarding, ambiguity |
| **Disagreeing with a senior person or another team** | conflict, communication |
| **Helping someone else grow** (senior+) | mentoring, leadership |

For each story, write down:

- One-sentence summary
- The numbers (scale, impact, before and after)
- Your specific actions
- What went wrong or what you'd do differently
- Which questions it answers

## Common mistakes

- **Only "we".** The interviewer can't tell what you did.
- **No numbers.** "It was much faster" is forgettable; "6 hours to 50 minutes" isn't.
- **No conflict or problem.** Stories where everything went smoothly don't show judgment.
- **Blaming others.** Talk about the system and the process, not people.
- **Stories that are too small for the level.** Senior candidates telling junior-sized stories get down-leveled.

## Practice

Each question has a rubric. Write or record your answer, then score it point by point.
