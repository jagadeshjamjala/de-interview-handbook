# Tool deep dives

Every tool on your resume is an invitation to a deep dive. Interviewers rarely ask for syntax. They ask **how the tool works underneath**, because that's what you need when it's slow, wrong or broken at 3 a.m.

<LevelExpectations
  junior="Explain what each tool on your resume is for, how you used it, and one thing that went wrong."
  mid="Explain the internals that matter for performance and correctness (partitions, shuffles, idempotency, pruning) for the tools you use daily."
  senior="Compare tools and their trade-offs, diagnose problems from symptoms, and know the cost model of each."
  lead="Choose between tools for a platform, and explain the operational and team costs of each choice."
/>

## How tool questions are asked

1. **"Walk me through how X works."** Architecture, from the write path to the read path.
2. **"This job is slow / wrong. What do you check?"** Diagnosis from symptoms: the most common format.
3. **"When would you not use X?"** Trade-offs and alternatives.
4. **"What does X cost, and how would you reduce it?"** Increasingly common.

The best preparation: for each tool on your resume, know its **unit of parallelism**, its **unit of storage**, how it **guarantees correctness** (or doesn't), and **what it bills for**.
