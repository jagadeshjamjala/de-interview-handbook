# Pipeline system design

"Design a pipeline that…" is the round that decides most senior offers. There's no single right answer. The interviewer wants to see you **turn vague requirements into a working, operable design**, and reason about trade-offs as constraints change.

## The design template

Every worked design here follows the same steps. Use them as your checklist in the interview.

1. **Requirements.** Who consumes the data, and for what decision? Freshness (seconds, minutes, daily)? Volume now and in two years? Correctness needs (approximate is fine vs money-exact)? History and retention?
2. **Data contracts.** What exactly does the source promise: schema, keys, ordering, delivery guarantees, how it signals deletes? Who is told when it changes?
3. **Ingestion.** Batch pull, push via API, a log or queue, or CDC. How do you get data in without hurting the source?
4. **Storage and format.** Raw (immutable, replayable) vs modeled. File format (Parquet), table format (Iceberg, Delta, Hudi) or warehouse tables. Partitioning and clustering.
5. **Transform.** Engine (SQL in the warehouse, Spark, Flink, dbt) and the modeled tables with their grain.
6. **Serving.** BI, an API, a feature store, reverse ETL. Latency and concurrency needs.
7. **Quality.** Tests at each layer, contracts, reconciliation against the source, anomaly alerts, and where bad data goes (quarantine).
8. **Orchestration and backfill.** Scheduling, dependencies, idempotent re-runs, how you backfill a month safely.
9. **Failure modes.** What breaks first? Source down, late data, duplicates, schema change, a bad deploy. How do you detect and recover?
10. **Cost.** What dominates the bill, and the dials you'd turn.
11. **What changes at 10×.** Volume, consumers or freshness. Which parts survive and which you'd redesign.

## Running the 45 minutes

| Minutes | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0–8 | Requirements and numbers. Write them on the board. Confirm the top two priorities. |
| 8–15 | High-level diagram, sources to consumers, five or six boxes. |
| 15–30 | Deep dive on the hardest part (usually ingestion semantics, late data or the core model). |
| 30–40 | Quality, failure modes, backfills, monitoring. |
| 40–45 | Cost, 10×, and what you'd do differently with more time. |

Ask the interviewer where they want depth. It's a good question, not a weakness.

## Numbers worth knowing

Rough figures for back-of-envelope estimates. They vary a lot by provider and configuration, so state them as assumptions.

- 1 billion events/day ≈ 11,600/second on average; plan for 3–5× peaks.
- At 1 KB per event, 1 billion events ≈ 1 TB/day raw JSON, often 5–10× smaller as compressed Parquet.
- A single Kafka partition comfortably handles thousands to tens of thousands of small messages per second. Choose partition counts for peak throughput and consumer parallelism.
- Small files hurt. Aim for Parquet files in the hundreds-of-MB range, and plan compaction.
