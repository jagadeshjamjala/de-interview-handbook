# The DE resume: quantify your pipelines

A recruiter spends under a minute on your resume, and a hiring manager scans it for one thing: **evidence you've built data systems that mattered**. Tool lists don't show that. Numbers do.

<LevelExpectations
  junior="One page. Projects and internships count. Show you built something end to end and measured it."
  mid="Show ownership: pipelines you ran, incidents you fixed, consumers you served, with numbers."
  senior="Show scope: systems that serve many teams, migrations, cost or reliability wins, people you made more effective."

## The four numbers DE managers look for

Every pipeline has these. Put at least one in most bullets:

| Dimension | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Volume** | 2B events/day, 40 TB, 300 tables, 5k writes/sec |
| **Latency / freshness** | nightly → 15 min, p95 query time 40s → 4s, SLA 07:00 |
| **Cost** | warehouse spend −35%, $180k/yr saved, cost per query |
| **Reliability / quality** | failed runs 12/month → 1, data incidents −80%, 99.9% on-time |

Add a fifth when you can: **who used it** ("used by 60 analysts", "feeds the pricing model").

## The bullet formula

**Action verb + what you built + how + measurable result.**

- ❌ "Worked on ETL pipelines using Airflow and Spark."
- ✅ "Rebuilt the nightly orders pipeline in Spark and Airflow with partition-level backfills, cutting runtime from 6h to 50 min and missed SLAs from 9 to 0 per month."

- ❌ "Responsible for data quality."
- ✅ "Added dbt tests and freshness alerts to 120 core models; caught 14 source breaks before they reached finance dashboards in the first quarter."

- ❌ "Migrated data warehouse to the cloud."
- ✅ "Led migration of 340 Teradata jobs to Snowflake and dbt with automated parity checks; decommissioned the legacy system a month early, saving $420k/yr."

If you don't know the exact number, estimate honestly and be ready to explain how ("about 2B events/day, from the Kafka topic metrics"). Never invent one.

## Structure

1. **Header:** name, location or remote, email, LinkedIn, GitHub (only if it has something worth showing).
2. **Summary (optional, two lines):** your role type and strongest area, for example "Platform data engineer, 6 years, batch and streaming on AWS; focus on reliability and cost."
3. **Experience:** 3–6 bullets per recent role, fewer for older ones. Most impressive first.
4. **Skills:** grouped and short. *Languages:* SQL, Python, Scala. *Processing:* Spark, Flink, dbt. *Storage:* Snowflake, Iceberg, Postgres. *Orchestration:* Airflow. *Cloud:* AWS. Only list what you'd be happy to be quizzed on.
5. **Projects** (junior, career switchers): see [portfolio projects](./portfolio-projects.mdx).

## What to cut

- Tools you used once. Each one invites a deep-dive question.
- Responsibilities without outcomes ("maintained pipelines").
- Buzzword chains ("big data, real-time, AI-driven, cloud-native").
- More than two pages, at any level.

## Tailoring

Match the [role type](../start-here/which-de-are-you.mdx). For an analytics engineer role, lead with modeling, dbt and stakeholder outcomes. For a platform role, lead with infra, reliability and cost. Same experience, different ordering.

## Prepare to defend every line

Each bullet is a question waiting to happen. For your top three bullets, be ready to explain the design, the trade-offs, what broke and what you'd do differently. The [behavioral page](../rounds/behavioral.mdx) shows how to turn them into stories.
