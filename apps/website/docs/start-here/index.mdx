# Start here

This is a **practice** handbook for data engineering interviews. You'll read less and do more: solve SQL on real tables in your browser, work through modeling and pipeline design prompts, and grade yourself against the same rubric an interviewer would use.

<LevelExpectations
  junior="Get fast and correct at SQL, explain your choices out loud, and show you can learn. Breadth over depth."
  mid="Own a pipeline end to end: SQL, modeling, orchestration, data quality. Explain trade-offs, not only solutions."
  senior="Design systems under constraints (scale, cost, late data, failure), and lead the conversation in design rounds."
  lead="Everything above, plus platform strategy, team design, and decisions that hold up for years. Use the behavioral stories to show scope."
/>

## The path

1. **[How DE interviews work](./how-de-interviews-work.mdx).** Know which rounds you'll face and what each one is really testing.
2. **[Which DE are you?](./which-de-are-you.mdx)** Analytics, platform, streaming and ML data roles weight the rounds differently. Spend your hours where they count.
3. **[Pick your level track](./pick-your-level.mdx).** Every page opens with a "what's expected at your level" box. Set your level in the navbar and your row is highlighted.
4. **[Make a study plan](./de75-planner.mdx)** with the DE 75 planner.
5. **Fix your resume** ([Step 1](../resume/de-resume.mdx)) before you apply, because it decides which loops you get.
6. **Practice the rounds** ([Step 2](../rounds/sql.mdx)). Start with SQL and data modeling; they decide most DE loops.

## How to practice

- **Try before you look.** Every hint and solution is hidden until you open it. Give each question a real attempt, timed if you can.
- **Run your SQL.** SQL questions have a **Try it in the SQL playground** button. It checks your result against the reference answer on the same data, right in your browser. The [playground](/playground) also lets you explore the datasets freely.
- **Grade yourself honestly.** Non-SQL questions come with a weighted rubric. Mark each point strong, partial or missed. The missed points are your study list.
- **Talk out loud.** In real interviews, how you reason counts as much as the answer. Practice explaining as you go.
